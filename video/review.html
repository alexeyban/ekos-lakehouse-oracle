01-intro EKOS on a legacy ERP: from analysis to reconciled marts

40.5 s · 132 words · 1 shot(s)

This is a recording of one real run, start to finish. The source is eye dempiere, an open source E R P on Oracle, with nine hundred tables, twenty years of migration scripts and a large Java code base. We create a new project in the Ekos console and take it through the seven steps of the operator workflow. Analyse the sources. Generate the documentation. Review and sign off the source model. Describe the reports we want. Approve how each table is loaded. Build bronze and silver. And build the gold marts, each one reconciled against silver. Nothing here is staged: every screen is from this run, and every number we say is on screen. Where something went wrong during the recording, we say so, and we show how it was fixed.

03-analyse Step 1 — Analyse the sources

05-scope Step 3 — Review: scope

34.6 s · 105 words · 5 shot(s)

Step three is the review, and it is a person's job; here, a demo operator. Six subjects: scope, personal data, conflicts, identity, write behaviour and glossary. Scope first. Ekos proposed eight hundred and forty nine tables as business data. This lakehouse is for the order to invoice slice, so one command from the console puts the other eight hundred and sixteen tables out of scope, with a reason. The review now shows the slice. Signing off means typing the subject's name: the sign off is bound to a digest of exactly what was shown, and turns stale by itself if a fact behind it changes.

06-conflicts Step 3 — Conflicts, identity and sign-off

33.8 s · 101 words · 5 shot(s)

Inside the slice, seventeen conflicts block the sign off. Each one is a column the application's model calls mandatory, while the database allows null. Ekos keeps both claims, with their sources, and measures the data: here, zero contradicting rows. The operator decides that both are valid, and says why. One identity question remains: a git handle and a display name. The handle is the local part of that person's commit address, so they are the same contributor. Personal data, write behaviour and the glossary are signed as well. Six of six subjects signed: steps four to seven are no longer blocked.

07-extraction Step 5 — The extraction plan

34.2 s · 104 words · 4 shot(s)

We continue with step five, not four, and that is a finding of this recording: requirements resolve against the deployed plan, so the plan has to be approved and generated first, even though the step bar suggests step four. The plan says, for each of the thirty three tables, how it lands in bronze. Incremental where the application's code keeps the updated column, full otherwise; how deletes are detected; which columns are masked. Every decision shows its evidence. The operator changes one table to a full load, with a reason, and approves the plan. Approval binds the plan's digest: nothing else can be generated.

08-requirements Step 6 → Step 4 — Generate, then describe the marts

39.0 s · 118 words · 4 shot(s)

Generate writes the pipeline: the extraction sequel, the D B T silver models with their tests, and the Dagster job. Now step four. Two marts are described as Markdown: sales by month and customer, and invoices by month. Every term resolves. The third is written in plain words: net sales of completed customer orders by month, product category and the customer's region. The local model, llama three, drafts the mart. This is the only language model call in the whole workflow, and Ekos does not trust it: it resolves every term against the approved model. Two terms fail. The measure is an expression the resolver does not accept, and the filter names a column that does not exist.

09-answers Step 4 — The operator answers; nothing is invented

27.3 s · 94 words · 4 shot(s)

Questions go to the operator, never back to the model. The operator replaces the measure with the sum of the order lines' net amount, and the filter with completed customer orders. Then Ekos asks a harder question: the customer's region can be reached from an order line along many different foreign key paths, each with a different meaning, and Ekos will not pick one. The operator names the path through the business partner's location. Every term now resolves, and the change log keeps each answer and who gave it. All three requirements are approved.

10-load Step 6 — Bronze and silver through Dagster

39.1 s · 118 words · 5 shot(s)

Run the pipeline. Dagster loads the thirty three tables from Oracle into bronze, in Iceberg, and builds silver with D B T. The run succeeded: thirty six steps in five minutes and three seconds, sixty eight assets and eight hundred and ninety five checks. Getting here took three launches, and this is the second finding. The new project's silver first landed in the schema an earlier project uses, because silver and gold schema names ignored the project's prefix. We stopped, fixed the product so each project gets its own silver and gold schemas, regenerated and reloaded. Two tables carry data warnings: a document type and a payment type hold a code the application's code list does not declare.

11-marts Step 7 — Marts: propose, merge, build, reconcile

34.7 s · 110 words · 6 shot(s)

Step seven turns each approved requirement into a gold mart. Propose compiles the description into a D B T model on a branch, and checks that nothing in it was invented. Merge is the operator's approval. Build runs only that mart and its tests through Dagster. Reconcile reads each measure's total from the mart and computes the same total from silver. Every term is exact, and every total matches: three hundred and fifty eight point four zero for net sales by region and category, three hundred and seventy nine point nine zero and three orders for sales by month. All three marts are reconciled, in the project's own gold schema.

12-lineage Models, transformations and lineage

29.1 s · 98 words · 4 shot(s)

D B T's own lineage graph shows the models: bronze sources on the left, the silver models, and the three gold marts on the right. Column level lineage goes further. For net sales, the order line's net amount is cast into bronze, the latest version is kept in silver, and gold sums it. Month comes from the order date, truncated to the month. Every step is read from the generated sequel, not drawn by hand. Making this work for Oracle's upper case names was the third fix of this recording: the documentation tool matched names with their case.

13-recap What we got

37.1 s · 110 words · 2 shot(s)

To sum up. One new project, analysed from code, documents and the live database with zero model calls. Two hundred and thirty nine pages of documentation published. A review signed off by a person, decision by decision. Three marts described in Markdown or in plain words, resolved without invention, built through Dagster and reconciled to the cent. What is still open: two code values outside the application's lists in silver, the dashboard's growth chart timing out on this ledger, and the step bar suggesting requirements before the plan.
